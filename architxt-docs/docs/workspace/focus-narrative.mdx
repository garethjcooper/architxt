# Focus: narrative

A **narrative** directive asks architxt to answer a question or explore a topic in plain prose. It is the most common output type in Reflect and the simplest to turn into a curated page.

## When to use a narrative focus

Use narrative when you want:

- A summary of entities, relationships, or documents.
- A walkthrough of a process or argument.
- An explanation in paragraphs with headings and lists.
- Content that is easy to edit, reorder, and save as Markdown.

## What a narrative envelope looks like

A narrative envelope is a document-style result. It is composed of sections that map directly to curated-page sections:

| Element | Typical content |
|---|---|
| **Title** | The question or topic the narrative answers. |
| **Headings** | Logical sections such as “Overview”, “Key entities”, “Implications”. |
| **Paragraphs** | Prose paragraphs that cite or describe the underlying data. |
| **Lists** | Bulleted or numbered lists for grouped facts. |

Narrative output is plain text first. Diagrams, tables, and graphs are produced by other directive focuses and can be combined with narrative on a curated page.

## Preview mode

In a Preview tab a narrative envelope is shown through the narrative viewer. The viewer renders headings, paragraphs, and lists and adds section-level controls.

| Control | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Index** | Adds paragraph/section numbers for easy citation. |
| **Plain** | Strips formatting to show bare text. |
| **Copy** | Copies the whole narrative or an individual section. |
| **Save as Markdown** | Exports the rendered narrative as `.md`. |

Narrative previews have no diagram source tab or table grid because the content is already text.

## Curated-page editing

When you copy a narrative section into a curated page, it becomes an editable text section. You can:

- Edit the prose directly in the section editor.
- Split or merge paragraphs.
- Reorder sections relative to diagrams, tables, and graphs.
- Soft-delete and restore sections until you save.

:::tip[Edit with the envelope in mind]
A narrative section remembers its source envelope. If the underlying query or mental model changes, you may want to re-copy the section rather than editing it by hand.
:::

## Consistency tips

To get predictable narrative results:

- Be explicit in the query about tone and depth: “summarise in three paragraphs” or “list the five most important points”.
- Use directives to constrain the answer when the bank contains many entities.
- Break large questions into smaller queries and combine the answers on a curated page.
